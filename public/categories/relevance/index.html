Fallacies in the Relevance category.

Ad Hominem

Attacking the person making an argument instead of addressing the argument itself.

Appeal to Authority

Treating an authority's claim as sufficient evidence that the claim must be true.

Appeal to Emotion

Using an emotional response as a substitute for evidence supporting a conclusion.

Appeal to Expertise

Treating an expert's opinion as decisive evidence without considering whether the expertise, evidence, or consensus supports the claim.

Appeal to Fear

Using fear of a negative consequence to persuade someone to accept a conclusion without adequate evidence.

Appeal to Nature

Assuming that something is good, safe, or desirable because it is natural, or bad because it is unnatural.

Appeal to Pity

Using sympathy or compassion as a substitute for evidence supporting a conclusion.

Appeal to Tradition

Claiming that something is correct, valuable, or necessary simply because it has traditionally been done that way.

Bandwagon

Claiming that a belief or practice is correct because many people accept or follow it.

Genetic Fallacy

Judging a claim solely by its origin rather than evaluating the claim itself.

Poisoning the Well

Discrediting a person or source in advance so that their future arguments will be dismissed regardless of their evidence.

Red Herring

Introducing an irrelevant issue to distract from the argument being discussed.

Straw Man

Misrepresenting someone's argument so that it is easier to attack.

Tu Quoque

Rejecting criticism by accusing the critic of the same behavior.

Whataboutism

Responding to criticism by pointing to another person's wrongdoing instead of addressing the original criticism.
