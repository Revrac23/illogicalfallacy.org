Appeal to Pity

Using sympathy or compassion as a substitute for evidence supporting a conclusion.

Category: Relevance
Also known as: Argumentum ad Misericordiam
Type: Informal fallacy

Definition

An appeal to pity attempts to establish a conclusion by provoking sympathy for someone rather than providing relevant evidence for the conclusion.

Example

“You should give me a passing grade because I’ve had a really difficult semester.”

The student’s circumstances may deserve compassion, but they do not by themselves establish that the student has met the requirements for passing.

Why It’s Fallacious

Sympathy can be morally relevant to some decisions, but feeling sorry for someone does not establish that a factual claim is true or that a particular conclusion follows.

How to Recognize It

Ask:

“Is sympathy being used instead of evidence relevant to the conclusion?”
