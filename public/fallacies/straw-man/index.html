Straw Man

Misrepresenting someone's argument so that it is easier to attack.

Category: Relevance
Also known as: Straw Person
Type: Informal fallacy

Definition

A straw man fallacy occurs when someone misrepresents another person’s argument, position, or claim and then attacks the distorted version instead of addressing the original argument.

Example

“You think we should reduce unnecessary military spending? So you want to leave the country completely defenseless.”

The original argument was about reducing unnecessary spending, not eliminating the military.

Why It’s Fallacious

The response attacks a position that the other person did not actually make. Even if the distorted version is easily defeated, that does not establish that the original argument is wrong.

How to Recognize It

Compare the response with the original claim. Ask:

“Did they actually argue that?”

If the response relies on an exaggerated, simplified, or distorted version of the original position, it may be a straw man.
