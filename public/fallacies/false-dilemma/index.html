False Dilemma

Presenting only two options when more possibilities exist.

Category: Presumption
Also known as: False Dichotomy, Either-Or Fallacy
Type: Informal fallacy

Definition

A false dilemma presents a situation as having only two possible choices when additional alternatives exist.

Example

“Either you support this proposal or you don’t care about the community.”

Someone could care about the community while disagreeing with the proposal.

Why It’s Fallacious

The argument artificially limits the available options. Rejecting one presented option does not necessarily mean accepting the other.

How to Recognize It

Look for language such as:

“Either A or B.”

Then ask:

“Are A and B really the only possibilities?”
