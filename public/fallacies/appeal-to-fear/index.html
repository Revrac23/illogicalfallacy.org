Appeal to Fear

Using fear of a negative consequence to persuade someone to accept a conclusion without adequate evidence.

Category: Relevance
Also known as: Argumentum ad Metum
Type: Informal fallacy

Definition

An appeal to fear attempts to establish a conclusion by emphasizing frightening consequences rather than providing sufficient evidence for the conclusion.

Example

“If you don’t support this surveillance program, criminals will take over the city.”

The frightening prediction does not establish that the surveillance program is necessary or effective.

Why It’s Fallacious

Fear can be a legitimate response to genuine danger, but the emotional reaction does not substitute for evidence that the proposed conclusion is justified.

How to Recognize It

Ask:

“What evidence supports the conclusion apart from the threat being described?”
