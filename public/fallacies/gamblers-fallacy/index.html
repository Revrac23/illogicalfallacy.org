Gambler's Fallacy

Believing that an independent random event is more or less likely because previous independent events had different outcomes.

Category: Probability
Also known as: Monte Carlo Fallacy
Type: Informal fallacy

Definition

The gambler’s fallacy is the mistaken belief that previous independent random events change the probability of a future independent event.

Example

“The coin has landed heads five times in a row, so tails is due next.”

If the coin is fair and the flips are independent, the previous results do not make tails more likely on the next flip.

Why It’s Fallacious

Independent random events do not remember previous outcomes.

How to Recognize It

Ask:

“Do previous outcomes actually affect the probability of the next outcome?”

If the events are independent, previous results do not change their underlying probabilities.
