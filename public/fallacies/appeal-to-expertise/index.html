Appeal to Expertise

Treating an expert's opinion as decisive evidence without considering whether the expertise, evidence, or consensus supports the claim.

Category: Relevance
Type: Informal fallacy

Definition

An appeal to expertise occurs when an expert’s opinion is treated as sufficient proof without adequately considering the expert’s relevant qualifications, evidence, or the degree of agreement among qualified experts.

Example

“A physicist says this historical claim is true, so it must be true.”

Expertise in physics does not automatically establish expertise in history.

Why It’s Fallacious

Expert testimony can be valuable evidence. The problem occurs when expertise is treated as infallible or when an expert’s credentials are unrelated to the claim.

How to Recognize It

Ask:

“Is this person actually an appropriate expert on this particular claim?”
