## Definition

The **base rate fallacy** occurs when someone focuses on specific evidence while neglecting the underlying frequency of the event or condition being considered.

## Example

Suppose a rare disease affects 1 in 10,000 people. A test is highly accurate, but a positive result occurs.

> "The test is 99% accurate, so there's a 99% chance this person has the disease."

That conclusion may ignore the very low base rate of the disease.

## Why It's Fallacious

Specific evidence needs to be interpreted alongside the underlying probability of the condition.

## How to Recognize It

Ask:

> "How common is this condition before considering the specific evidence?"
