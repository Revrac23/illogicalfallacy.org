## Definition

The **ambiguity fallacy** occurs when an argument depends on a statement that can reasonably be interpreted in multiple ways, without establishing which meaning is intended.

## Example

> "The sign says the store is for everyone, so anyone can enter at any time."

The phrase "for everyone" does not necessarily mean the store is open at all times.

## Why It's Fallacious

An argument cannot rely on an interpretation that has not been established when other reasonable interpretations are possible.

## How to Recognize It

Ask:

> "Could this statement reasonably mean more than one thing?"

If the argument depends on one particular interpretation, that interpretation needs to be established.
