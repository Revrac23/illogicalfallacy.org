## Definition

The **hot-hand fallacy** is the belief that someone who has recently succeeded is more likely to succeed again because they are "on a streak," even when the outcomes are independent.

## Example

> "She's made five three-point shots in a row, so she is definitely more likely to make the next one."

A streak alone does not necessarily change the underlying probability of the next shot.

## Why It's Fallacious

Past success does not automatically increase the probability of future success when outcomes are independent.

## How to Recognize It

Ask:

> "Does the previous streak actually affect the probability of the next outcome?"
