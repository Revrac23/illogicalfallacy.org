## Definition

**Appealing to expertise** means using someone's specialist judgment to support a claim. It can provide good evidence when the expertise fits the claim and the conclusion stays within what the expert's assessment supports. New evidence can change how much weight that judgment deserves. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Fallacies, §3.6](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/#DiaAppFal)

## Fallacious Example

> A building scientist says that poor ventilation could explain the damp patches in our office. That proves ventilation caused them, so there’s no need to check for leaks.

The scientist has relevant expertise and offered a possible explanation. The speaker turns that tentative judgment into a definitive diagnosis.

## Legitimate Example

> The building scientist inspected the office, reviewed its humidity logs, and checked for leaks. She says poor ventilation is the most likely cause, so we’ll start by checking the ventilation system.

The assessment draws on observations from the office, and the next step reflects the uncertainty in the finding.

## How It Relates to Appeal to Authority

The labels overlap. [Appeal to Authority](/fallacies/appeal-to-authority/) emphasizes how status or reputation can carry undue weight. This page emphasizes how to assess and use a specialist's judgment. Both can describe cases where someone relies on credentials outside the relevant field. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Fallacies, §1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/#CorFal)

## How to Recognize It

Ask whether the expert works on this kind of problem, what evidence they considered, and whether the argument preserves the limits of their conclusion.
