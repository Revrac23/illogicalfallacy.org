## Definition

The [burden of proof](/fallacies/burden-of-proof/) is the responsibility to support a claim. Someone **shifts the burden of proof** when they unfairly transfer that responsibility to another person, such as by demanding that a skeptic disprove an unsupported assertion. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Fallacies, §3.6](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/#DiaAppFal)

## Fallacious Example

> This lighting retrofit will cut the building’s electricity use by 30 percent. Unless your team can prove otherwise, you should accept that estimate.

The vendor owes evidence for the projected savings. Asking the customer to rule them out leaves the estimate unsupported. Treating the lack of disproof as evidence for the estimate also makes this an [Appeal to Ignorance](/fallacies/appeal-to-ignorance/). [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Fallacies, §2.4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/#Loc)

## Legitimate Example

> The vendor shares an estimate based on a fixture inventory, operating hours, and recent utility bills. The facilities manager says the operating hours are too high, so the vendor asks to see the manager’s shift schedules.

The vendor has offered support for its estimate. The manager has introduced a specific counterclaim about operating hours, and the schedules can help both sides evaluate it. The vendor remains responsible for the savings estimate.

## How to Recognize It

Ask whether the original claim has support and whether the other person has made a claim of their own or is simply asking for evidence.
